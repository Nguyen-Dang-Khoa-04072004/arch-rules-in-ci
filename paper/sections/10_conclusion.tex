\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper offers one of the first large-scale, multi-ecosystem longitudinal studies of executable architecture rules in open-source software. Our results show that architecture-as-code is real but uneven: adoption remains limited, rule intent is concentrated around dependency and layering concerns, rule files evolve through repeated exemptions and relaxation, and rule adoption can improve structural outcomes when it is implemented with disciplined enforcement. We formalize a concept of \rrot and show that the observable existence of a rule file is not equivalent to the effective governance of architecture.

The benchmark released under \ArchRules provides a common data foundation for future studies of architecture enforcement, rule drift, and governance experiments. We hope it enables the research community to move from anecdotal examples to a more complete, empirically grounded understanding of how executable architecture checks shape software evolution over time. Future work should explore richer causal mechanisms, extend the benchmark to additional ecosystems, and integrate AaC with the governance needs of AI-assisted development and architecture-aware automation.
