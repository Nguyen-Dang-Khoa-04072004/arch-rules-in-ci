\begin{abstract}
Executable architecture conformance checks are 
widely advocated as a means of preventing architectural 
erosion, 
yet empirical evidence concerning their adoption, 
enforcement, and long-term evolution remains limited. 
We conduct a large-scale empirical study of ArchUnit, 
import-linter, and dependency-cruiser across 4,300 GitHub 
repositories. Our dataset comprises 4,321 repository tool 
observations, 11,098 rule files, and 86,339 historical 
edits. We normalize architectural rule constructs into 
a common taxonomy, examine GitHub Actions workflow evidence, 
and reconstruct rule histories to investigate changes in 
enforcement over time.
Our results reveal substantial differences across tools 
and ecosystems. GitHub Actions invocation is 
observed in 42.5\% of import-linter observations and 
11.6\% of dependency-cruiser observations. 
For ArchUnit, 35.9\% of observations contain potentially 
relevant Maven or Gradle build-or-test evidence, 
which serves as a proxy rather than direct evidence of architectural rule execution. Dependency and layering constraints account for 98.1\% of classified import-linter constructs, compared with 58.5\% for ArchUnit and 56.8\% for dependency-cruiser. The estimated 24-month cumulative incidence of the first exemption or disabling event is 12.9\%, 58.1\%, and 80.5\%, respectively. Furthermore, 49.6\% of rule files receive no subsequent modifications during their observed histories.
Finally, an analysis of ArchUnit adoption reveals no 
consistent decline in architectural violation counts 
after adoption. Among 141 repositories with complete 
three-point trajectories, violation counts do not 
exhibit a consistent downward trend. These findings 
show that defining architectural constraints, 
integrating checks into CI, and maintaining effective 
enforcement are distinct aspects of architectural 
governance. Our study provides large-scale empirical 
evidence of these differences and motivates further 
investigation into the long-term evolution and enforcement 
of executable architecture constraints.
\end{abstract}
