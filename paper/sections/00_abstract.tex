\begin{abstract}
Although executable checks are widely advocated as an approach
to enforcing architectural constraints, empirical evidence 
remains limited regarding their real-world use, 
the types of rules they encode, and their long-term 
effects.
To address this gap, we analyze 4,300 GitHub
repositories containing artifacts from ArchUnit, import-linter, 
and dependency-cruiser.
We exclude forks, archived repositories, and templates, 
and require each repository to have at least six months 
between its creation and latest push, with rules introduced 
between 2017 and 2026.
We then normalize rules into a canonical schema and 
reconstruct the histories of 11,098 rule files. 
Our analysis reveals differences across tools in 
workflow integration, rule content, and rule evolution.
For workflow integration, 42.5\% of import-linter 
repositories and 11.6\% of dependency-cruiser 
repositories explicitly reference the checker in 
a GitHub Actions workflow, while 35.9\% of ArchUnit 
repositories integrate ArchUnit into their build
or test workflows.
The tools also differ in the types of architectural 
rules they enforce. Dependency and layering constraints 
make up 98.1\% of import-linter rules, 
compared with 58.5\% of ArchUnit rules and 56.8\% of 
dependency-cruiser rules. Cycle detection accounts 
for 43.2\% of dependency-cruiser rules, while ArchUnit 
rules more commonly focus on naming and annotation 
constraints.
We define \textit{rule rot} to describe the exemption
or disabling of architectural checks in a repository. 
The estimated 24-month 
cumulative incidence is 26.3\% overall, 
ranging from 12.9\% for ArchUnit to 58.1\% 
for import-linter and 80.5\% for dependency-cruiser. 
At the same time, 49.6\% of rule files 
remain unmodified throughout their observed 
histories.
