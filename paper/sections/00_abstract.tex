\begin{abstract}
Executable architecture conformance checks are widely advocated as a means of preventing architectural erosion, yet empirical evidence concerning their adoption, enforcement, and long-term evolution remains limited. We conduct a large-scale empirical study of ArchUnit, import-linter, and dependency-cruiser across 4,300 GitHub repositories. Our dataset comprises 4,321 repository–tool observations, 11,098 rule files, and 86,339 historical edits. We normalize architecture-rule constructs into a common taxonomy, examine GitHub Actions workflow evidence, and reconstruct rule histories to investigate changes in enforcement over time.
Our results reveal substantial differences across tools. Direct workflow invocation is observed in 42.5\% of import-linter observations and 11.6\% of dependency-cruiser observations, while 35.9\% of ArchUnit observations contain a potentially relevant Maven or Gradle build-or-test proxy. Dependency and layering constraints account for 98.1\% of classified import-linter constructs, compared with 58.5\% for ArchUnit and 56.8\% for dependency-cruiser. The estimated 24-month incidence of the first exemption or disabling event is 12.9\%, 58.1\%, and 80.5\%, respectively. Overall, 49.6\% of rule files are never modified after introduction during their observed histories.
Finally, among 298 ArchUnit repositories with pre-adoption and adoption observations, 80.5\% already satisfy the evaluated dependency and layering constraints before adoption, whereas 2.3\% eliminate detectable pre-existing violations at adoption. Among 141 repositories with complete three-point trajectories, violation counts do not exhibit a consistent decline after adoption. These findings characterize differences in architectural rule adoption and enforcement across ecosystems and highlight the need for further research into the long-term governance of executable architecture constraints.
\end{abstract}
