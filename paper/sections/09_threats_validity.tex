\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\subsection{Construct Validity}
\label{sec:threats-construct}

CI evidence is text in YAML files under \texttt{.github/} on the
default branch. import-linter matches \texttt{lint-imports} or
\texttt{import-linter}. dependency-cruiser matches \texttt{depcruise}
or \texttt{dependency-cruiser}. ArchUnit has no dedicated workflow
command. Its indicator is a Maven \texttt{test}, \texttt{verify},
\texttt{install}, or \texttt{package} task, or a Gradle \texttt{test},
\texttt{check}, or \texttt{build} task. The proxy records that a build
or test task is named. A workflow can name that task without executing
an ArchUnit rule.

Category labels come from a fixed automatic map onto seven categories.
The map was frozen on the first 1{,}000 eligible repository--tool rows.
Constructs from that development set are held out, and the proportions
use the remaining 21{,}713 constructs. Of those, 2{,}901 are
uncategorized and are omitted from the proportions. The gold set is
empty: the labels were not manually assigned, and we report no Cohen's
$\kappa$. The shares are the output of that map on the held-out
constructs that received a category.

Rule rot is the first edit that exempts or disables a check. The edit
may introduce a \texttt{FreezingArchRule}, an \texttt{@ArchIgnore}, an
\texttt{ignore\_imports} list, or a dependency-cruiser exception; it
may drop severity; or it may delete or comment out a rule. The
timestamp marks that edit and leaves the author's intent
unobserved. Restructure is the residual edit class. Every revision other than disable, exempt, loosen, tighten, or
add falls into it, including revisions whose normalized rule text is
unchanged. The class therefore mixes edits that alter the parsed
architecture with edits that leave the parsed rules as they were.

The adoption replay in Section~\ref{sec:rq4} evaluates dependency and
layering constraints on source-level import graphs. Cycle, naming,
annotation, visibility, and other rules lie outside the replay. The
procedure reads those graphs. It does not execute ArchUnit.

\subsection{Internal Validity}
\label{sec:threats-internal}

The CI shares, category proportions, and rule-rot incidence summarize
the workflows and rule files that were observed. They are not estimates
of the effect of adopting a checker.

The adoption analysis is descriptive in the same sense.
Section~\ref{sec:rq4} retains 317 of the 2{,}422 ArchUnit rows, those
with at least 12 months of history on each side of the first parseable
ArchUnit rule, and it replays dependency and layering only. Within
that set, 141 repositories have violation counts one month before
adoption, at adoption, and 12 months afterward. There is no untreated
control group, no matching, and no difference-in-differences design,
and the analysis estimates no average treatment effect. Other commits
in the same window can move the violation counts. The series describe
repositories that already contain a parseable ArchUnit rule and that
meet the history requirement.

\subsection{External Validity}
\label{sec:threats-external}

Repository discovery uses GitHub code search for tool manifests. The
hits are public projects indexed at query time, so private, deleted,
and unindexed projects are absent. The eligibility screen then removes
forks, archived repositories, templates and scaffolds, and repositories
with less than six months between creation and the latest push.
Short-lived projects and templates therefore drop out of every
proportion. Repositories with fewer than two contributors, and
repositories without a tag, remain when they pass that screen. The
findings describe ArchUnit, import-linter, and dependency-cruiser on
that public set. Other conformance tools, other forges, and proprietary
projects lie outside this evidence. Workflow evidence is GitHub Actions
only, and only YAML under \texttt{.github/} on the default branch. A
Jenkinsfile, a GitLab CI configuration, or a workflow that exists only
on another branch is unobserved.
