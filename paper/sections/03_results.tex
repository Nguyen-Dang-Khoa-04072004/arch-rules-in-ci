\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The analyses use 4{,}321 repository--tool rows from 4{,}300 repositories,
11{,}098 rule files, and 86{,}339 edits.
Sections~\ref{sec:rq1}--\ref{sec:rq4} report each research question in turn:
an exhibit of the locked comparison estimates, a short interpretation against
the corresponding hypothesis, and a key finding.

\subsection{RQ1: CI Integration}
\label{sec:rq1}

Table~\ref{tab:ci_results} and Figure~\ref{fig:ci_share} give the share of
repository--tool rows whose GitHub Actions workflows meet the tool-specific
integration test on the default branch.
Import-linter is invoked in 351 of 826 rows (42.5\%; 95\% CI 39.4--45.8\%).
Dependency-cruiser is invoked in 125 of 1{,}073 rows (11.6\%; 95\% CI 9.8--13.5\%).
Both intervals lie entirely below 50\%, so H1.1 holds for each tool.
Pooling the two tools yields 25.1\% (95\% CI 23.1--27.1\%), again entirely
below the threshold.
ArchUnit has no dedicated workflow command.
A Maven or Gradle build-or-test proxy is present in 869 of 2{,}421 rows with a
workflow result (35.9\%; 95\% CI 34.1--37.9\%).
That proxy records a potentially relevant build or test task; it does not
establish that ArchUnit rules ran, and it lies outside H1.1.

The cross-tool contrast is large: import-linter is nearly four times as likely
as dependency-cruiser to appear in a workflow command, while ArchUnit's proxy
sits between them.
The gap is consistent with tool-specific invocation conventions---dedicated
commands for import-linter and dependency-cruiser versus a build-or-test proxy
for ArchUnit---rather than with a uniform CI adoption rate across ecosystems.

\subsection{RQ2: Parsed Constructs}
\label{sec:rq2}

Classification uses the 21{,}713 held-out constructs summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:rule_taxonomy}.
Parse coverage among identified constructs is 84.7\% for ArchUnit
(15{,}173/17{,}907), 94.3\% for import-linter (2{,}136/2{,}265), and
97.5\% for dependency-cruiser (1{,}503/1{,}541).
The 2{,}901 uncategorized constructs are omitted from the proportions that
follow.
Figure~\ref{fig:categories_by_tool} shows the category mix by tool.

Dependency and layering account for 98.1\% of parsed import-linter constructs
(95\% CI 97.5--98.6\%), 58.5\% of parsed ArchUnit constructs (57.8--59.3\%),
and 56.8\% of parsed dependency-cruiser constructs (54.4--59.1\%).
Only the import-linter interval lies entirely above 70\%, so H2.1 holds for
import-linter only.
Among parsed dependency-cruiser constructs, cycle constraints are 649 of
1{,}503 (43.2\%).
Naming and annotation are 18.5\% and 8.4\% of parsed ArchUnit constructs;
visibility and other together add another 11.5\%.
The category mix differs by tool ($\chi^{2} = 5865.56$, $df = 12$,
Cram\'er's $V = 0.395$).

The pattern matches what each tool is built to express.
Import-linter contracts are almost entirely dependency and layering checks.
Dependency-cruiser splits roughly evenly between dependency edges and cycle
freedom.
ArchUnit alone carries a substantial share of naming and annotation
constraints, so a structural-family majority below 70\% does not imply thin
rule content---it implies a broader constraint vocabulary.
Because category labels are automatic and unvalidated against human
annotations, the shares describe the output of the fixed map rather than a
manually coded gold standard.

\begin{keyfinding}
Only import-linter concentrates dependency and layering above 70\%, so H2.1
holds for that tool alone; the category mix differs strongly across tools
(Cram\'er's $V = 0.395$).
\end{keyfinding}

\subsection{RQ3: Rule Rot}
\label{sec:rq3}

Rule rot is the first exempt or disable edit of a rule file
(Section~\ref{sec:background}).
Table~\ref{tab:rule_rot_by_tool} and Figure~\ref{fig:survival_analysis}
report the 24-month Kaplan--Meier incidence and the never-modified share.
The pooled incidence at 24 months is 26.3\% (95\% CI 25.4--27.2\%), below the
30\% threshold in H3.1.
The complete-case share among 5{,}122 files followed for 24 months is 49.3\%
(95\% CI 48.0--50.7\%) and is separate from that test: it conditions on long
follow-up and therefore cannot overturn the Kaplan--Meier result.
By tool, the 24-month incidence is 12.9\% for ArchUnit (8{,}579 files;
95\% CI 12.1--13.8\%), 58.1\% for import-linter (1{,}093 files; 54.0--62.3\%),
and 80.5\% for dependency-cruiser (1{,}426 files; 78.4--82.7\%).

After the introducing commit, 5{,}501 of 11{,}098 rule files (49.6\%;
95\% CI 48.6--50.4\%) are never modified.
The share is below the 73.8\% reported for committed AI-configuration
artifacts~\cite{denisov2026ase}, so H3.2 holds.
The never-modified share is 55.7\% for ArchUnit, 13.9\% for import-linter,
and 40.2\% for dependency-cruiser.

Table~\ref{tab:edit_class_counts} shows the automatic edit-class distribution.
Restructure is the residual class and covers 69{,}047 of the 86{,}339 edits
(80.0\%).
Exempt and disable together are 4.7\% of edits, yet they define the rule-rot
event when they first appear in a file's history.
A Cox model for time to the first loosen or exempt edit gives a hazard ratio
of 1.307 (95\% CI 1.270--1.345) for the log file-level commit count and
0.850 (0.793--0.911) for the log author count.
The Kaplan--Meier incidence of that broader maintenance event at 24 months is
41.9\% (95\% CI 40.9--43.0\%).

The pooled H3.1 miss is therefore compatible with severe tool-level rot:
ArchUnit pulls the average down, while dependency-cruiser and import-linter
cross the 30\% line by a wide margin.
H3.2 remains supported because nearly half of all rule files are never
touched after introduction, even though exemption and disabling are common
in some tools.
Edit classes are automatic and unvalidated; restructure mixes changes that
alter parsed rules with revisions that leave them unchanged.

\subsection{RQ4: Adoption Trajectories}
\label{sec:rq4}

Adoption is the first commit that introduces a parseable ArchUnit rule.
Of the 2{,}422 ArchUnit rows, 317 have at least 12 months of history on each
side of that commit.
The replay evaluates dependency and layering constraints only.
Mean coverage of that family is 52.9\%, and mean evaluable coverage is 33.5\%.
Among 298 repositories observed one month before adoption and at adoption,
240 (80.5\%) are ratifiers and 7 (2.3\%) are reformers.

Table~\ref{tab:rq4_trajectories} and Figure~\ref{fig:rq4_change} summarize
counts at one month before adoption, at adoption, and 12 months afterward for
141 of the 148 repositories with at least one translated rule.
The median count is 0 at each point; 83 of the 141 series are already zero at
$t{=}{-}1$.
The means are 73.3 (95\% CI 23.9--138.2), 85.6 (28.6--154.8), and 103.9
(33.8--196.2).
From the first of these points to the last, 18 series decrease (12.8\%;
95\% CI 7.1--17.7\%), 86 are unchanged (61.0\%; 53.2--69.5\%), and 37 increase
(26.2\%; 19.1--33.3\%).
Within-repository ordinary least squares gives a mean slope of 1.63 violations
per month in the pre-adoption year (95\% CI $-0.09$ to 4.38) and 1.83 in the
following year (0.47 to 3.74).

Most adopters with a complete window therefore ratify an already-clean
evaluable structure rather than reform a dirty one, and most complete series
do not fall after adoption.
The rising mean alongside a zero median indicates a heavy right tail rather
than a uniform deterioration.
These figures describe repositories that already adopted ArchUnit and that
satisfy the history and evaluability filters.
There is no untreated control group and no average treatment effect.
